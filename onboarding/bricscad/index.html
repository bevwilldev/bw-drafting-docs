Introduction to BricsCAD

BricsCAD is the CAD package the office draws in. If you've used AutoCAD you'll find it immediately familiar — same commands, same file format, same way of working.

Where it fits

Survey data comes in from the field, is processed, and arrives as points, strings and surfaces. From there the work diverges — a plan drafted and annotated to our standards, a Works-As-Executed set marked up against the design, an engineering detail dimensioned and plotted — but it all passes through BricsCAD and it all goes out as PDF. The plugin suite exists to make that middle step fast and consistent, whichever of those you're doing.

Coming from AutoCAD

BricsCAD uses the same DWG format, so files move between the two without conversion. Command names, aliases and the general interface are close enough that most habits carry over directly. The differences you're most likely to notice are in the settings dialogs and some of the more specialised toolsets.

Other software in the business

Not everyone works in BricsCAD. Survey processing and design work often happen in other packages, and drawings arrive as exports from those. It's worth knowing what a file has been through before you start editing it.
